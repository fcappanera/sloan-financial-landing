# Design research: 110 financial advisor homepages

Five segments, about 22 sites each, reviewed from real 1440px screenshots plus extracted nav, headings, CTAs and footers (October 2026).

| Segment | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| National boutique RIAs | Mission Wealth, Creative Planning, HB Wealth, Moneta, Abacus |
| Agency "best advisor websites" lists | Marrero, Archer, Peltoma, Brighton Jones, Appalachian Wealth |
| Solo and small CFP practices | Incline, Trailhead (Edina), RM Shannon (St. Paul), BKFi |
| Twin Cities and Burnsville competitors | Wealth Enhancement Burnsville, Laurel, 360 Financial, Great River |
| Tax plus planning firms | United FPG, Brickley, Hilltop, Compound, SSC |

## What most sites do

- Navy or another deep color plus one warm accent (about 70%).
- Client login in the header (77%), as a text link, never the main button.
- One conversational CTA repeated in the header, hero and closing band ("Schedule a conversation").
- "Who we serve" by audience (60 to 70%).
- Compliance links in the footer: Form CRS, ADV, BrokerCheck, disclosure paragraph (over 90%).

## What the best 20% do differently

- Split or calm hero: specific serif H1 of 8 to 10 words, one primary button, one text link. No sliders or video.
- H1 names who they help and where. Only 4 of 23 local competitors put the city in the H1.
- Thin trust strip under the hero instead of testimonials.
- Numerals or 20 to 28px line icons. Icons of 60px and up read as a template.
- Real people early: founder note, team headshots.
- Tax plus wealth firms show integration explicitly; a comparison table was the strongest device seen.
- Local proof: address in the hero, both offices, map, communities served, FAQ with schema.

## Common mistakes avoided

Sliders, oversized clip-art icons, stock handshakes, cookie or popup overlays, 20+ item menus, vague H1s, phone-only CTAs, and scroll animations that leave sections blank.
